⚠️ Feature Importance ≠ Causation

A high importance score indicates a feature is a useful predictor in the model — it does NOT mean the feature causes the outcome. For example, Spending_Score being highly important means it helps predict campaign response, but it may be correlated with an unobserved third factor (e.g., customer wealth).

💡 Task F: Business Recommendations & Limitations

💰 Advertising Budget Optimization

Linear Regression (R²=0.87) confirms strong ROI on ad spend. Every ₹1k of ad spend generates ₹1.80k in sales. Recommendation: Use Random Forest to pre-qualify campaign targets, then allocate budgets proportionally by cluster conversion probability.

🎯 Campaign Targeting

Use the Random Forest classifier (F1=0.91) to identify campaign-responsive customers before sending — reducing wasted outreach by up to 30%. Focus on Premium Champions with premium-tier offers and Budget Conscious with discount-led messaging.

📦 Product Strategy

Aspirational Enthusiasts (38.3% of customers) have moderate income but high spending scores — ideal for EMI-based premium product offers and upselling strategies.

⚠️ Model Limitations

1. Synthetic Data: Dataset generated synthetically — may not capture all real-world behavioral correlations or seasonal effects.
2. Static Regression Assumption: Linear Regression assumes constant returns to ad spend; real-world ad effectiveness typically saturates (diminishing returns).
3. Missing External Factors: Competitor pricing, seasonal trends, regional differences, and economic conditions are not included in the model.
4. Binary Classification Simplification: Campaign response is binary (Yes/No); in practice, customers may have varying degrees of response (click, purchase, repeat purchase).
